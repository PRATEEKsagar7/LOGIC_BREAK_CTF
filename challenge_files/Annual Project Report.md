## Annual Project Report

The project began as an experimental study of simulated environments and human decision-making. A small specialist team was assembled because ordinary methods could not reach the target directly.

The team designed multiple artificial environments, with each environment operating inside another. Every deeper layer changed the flow of time, so a few minutes in one place could represent hours somewhere else.

During the operation, the team discovered that the lead specialist's memories were affecting the simulated environments. People and locations from his past began appearing without warning, creating unexpected obstacles.

The final stage was described internally as an **inception**, where an idea had to be placed into the target's mind without the target realizing that it had been introduced.

The team eventually completed the operation, but the experience raised a difficult question: when every layer looks real, how can anyone be certain that they have truly returned?

The project demonstrated that perception, memory, and reality can become difficult to separate when people are given enough control over what they experience.
